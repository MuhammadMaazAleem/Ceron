### 3. Deep Conceptual Explanation
Three modes of heat transfer: Conduction (free electron diffusion & lattice vibrations in solids, thermal conductivity k), Convection (fluid currents, land and sea breezes), and Radiation (infrared electromagnetic waves, Stefan-Boltzmann law). Everyday applications: thermos flask, solar water heaters, and the greenhouse effect.

### 4. Key Terms, Definitions & Formulas
- **Thermal Conductivity** (حرارتی ایصالیت): Measure of ability of a material to conduct heat: Q/t = k A (T1 - T2) / L.
- **Greenhouse Effect** (گرین ہاؤس اثر): Trapping of longer wavelength infrared radiation by atmospheric gases (CO2, H2O vapor).

```
Rate of Conduction: Rate = (k · A · ΔT) / L
Heat conducted per unit time.
```

### 6. Worked Examples

#### Example 1: Heat Conduction Through a Glass Window
**Problem:** A glass sheet of area 2 m² and thickness 0.005 m has a temperature difference of 10°C across it. If k = 0.8 W/m·K, find the heat conduction rate.
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
- **Step 1: Formula:** Q/t = k A ΔT / L.
- **Step 2: Calculation:** Rate = 0.8 × 2 × 10 / 0.005 = 16 / 0.005 = 3200 Watts.
**Answer:** `Heat Conduction Rate = 3200 Joules/second (3.2 kW)`


### 7. Common Mistakes & Exam Traps

- ❌ **Mistake:** Stating that radiation requires a material medium.
  ✓ **Correction:** Radiation travels via electromagnetic waves and can propagate through a vacuum (e.g., solar energy reaching Earth).
  *Reason: Conduction and convection need matter; radiation does not.*
